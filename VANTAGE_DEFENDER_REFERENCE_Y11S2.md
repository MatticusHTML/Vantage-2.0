## ⚠️ Y11S2 CHANGES THAT MATTER (defense side, read first)

- **Mozzie** — Pest capture range increased to **1.75m** (from 1.5m). Easier to intercept attacker drones and cover single doorways without pixel-perfect aim.
- **Pulse** — Cardiac Sensor scan distance increased to **10.5m** (from 9m). Stronger horizontal intel — he reads more of the floor before committing.
- **Solis** — SPEC-IO and Overclock duration increased to **17s** (from 15s), with less restrictive resource management. Less timing pressure when scanning.
- **Cross-side threat (Dokkaebi remaster):** her new **Jegeo Payload** targets a single defender's phone and, if ignored, explodes it (40 HP + fire). A destroyed phone **permanently kills that defender's camera access** — this directly hits **Maestro, Echo, and Skopós**. Cam-reliant anchors must now respect Dokkaebi and dismiss the call or reposition.
- **Ranked 3.0:** no hidden MMR, visible rank = skill rating, placement matches, same-rank matchmaking. Stat reads are cleaner — trust the visible rank.
- New map **Calypso Casino** (heavy verticality — rooftop crane, basement spawn, expanded vents). Anchors must respect more vertical and flank angles than usual; roamers gain new routes.

## 📏 COACHING RULES (apply to every entry below)

1. **Recommended role is guidance, not law.** A roaming anchor, an aggressive Mozzie, a fragging Bandit — all valid if the data supports them.
2. **If an unconventional style is winning, leave it alone.** High win rate on an off-role build is a strength, not an error.
3. **When stats are bad, coach the habit — never the person.** Give an actionable fix.
4. **Stay in side.** Never recommend swapping a defender for an attacker or comparing across the ATK/DEF line.
5. **Anchors and trappers win rounds with low K/D.** Judge setup ops by site holds, plant denials, time bought, gadgets that forced attacker utility, and intel — not by frags. Judge roamers and duelists by frags *and* by whether they actually drained the attack clock.

### Alibi
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** Mx4 Storm, ACS12
**Secondary weapons:** Bailiff 410, Keratos .357
**Gadget:** Prisma — deployable holographic decoys of herself; any attacker who shoots or touches one is pinged for the team.
**Recommended role:** Intel roamer / flex
**Recommended playstyle:** Place Prisma decoys on common entry lines and approach angles so pushing attackers reveal themselves. Roam off the pings, catching attackers who just lit themselves up. Don't bunch decoys — spread them across the routes attackers actually use. Winning looks like attackers entering blind and getting traded the moment they shoot a hologram.
**Flex note:** Anchoring with Alibi (decoys on site doorways for plant-denial intel) is a perfectly valid alternative to roaming, especially on tight sites.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Intel + frag — the Mx4 frags well, so K/D is partly fair, but value the pings generated and roamers/entries she catches off them. Bad habit: decoys clustered where no one walks, or placed in plain sight where attackers instantly know they're fake. Good habit: decoys on real lines that bait shots and feed the team kills.

### Aruni
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** P10 RONI, Mk 14 EBR
**Secondary weapons:** PRB92
**Gadget:** Surya Gate — laser gates deployed on doorframes, windows, and hatches that damage attackers who pass through and destroy attacker gadgets/projectiles crossing them.
**Recommended role:** Anti-entry / anti-gadget anchor (flexes to aggressive site defender)
**Recommended playstyle:** Gate the doorways and hatches attackers must cross to reach site, forcing them to shoot the gate (noisy, slow) or take damage. The Mk 14 EBR is a hard-hitting DMR that lets her hold long angles aggressively. Place gates where they protect the plant and your angle simultaneously. Winning looks like an attacker chunked or delayed at every entry while you punish from a held angle.
**Flex note:** Aruni is strong played **aggressively** — the EBR rewards proactive angle-holding and peeking, not just passive anchoring. If a player is winning gunfights with the EBR, that aggression is a feature, not a flaw.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Anchor with real frag upside — judge both. Round impact (entries denied, gadgets destroyed, plant protected) matters, *and* the EBR means weak frags aren't fully excused. Bad habit: gating useless openings, or holding so passively the EBR's damage goes to waste. Good habit: gates on the real entry points plus confident EBR trades.

### Azami
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** 9x19VSN, ACS12
**Secondary weapons:** D-50
**Gadget:** Kiba Barrier — thrown kunai that expand into a bulletproof barrier on impact, patching holes, sealing sightlines, or creating cover on the fly.
**Recommended role:** Reactive flex / anchor
**Recommended playstyle:** Hold barriers for *reactive* use — patch a breached wall, seal an opened hatch, or create cover to win a 1vX or cover the plant denial. Don't dump all charges at setup; their power is responding to what the attack does. The 9x19VSN frags well, so she duels effectively. Winning looks like an attacker's breach or opening neutralized at the exact moment it mattered.
**Flex note:** Aggressive Azami — using barriers offensively to create new angles to peek attackers from — is a valid high-skill style. If the duels are landing, encourage it.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Flex with strong frag potential — K/D is fairly weighed, but her unique value is reactive barriers that save rounds. Bad habit: blowing all barriers at round start for static cover that gets bypassed. Good habit: holding charges to answer the breach/plant, plus winning gunfights with the VSN.

### Bandit
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** MP7, M870
**Secondary weapons:** P12
**Gadget:** Shock Wire (CED-1 batteries) — electrifies reinforced walls, hatches, barbed wire, and deployable shields, destroying anything touching them and denying hard breach.
**Recommended role:** Anti-hard-breach anchor / aggressive roamer
**Recommended playstyle:** Battery the reinforced wall the attackers want to breach and "Bandit-trick" — re-apply batteries during their breach attempt to keep the charge from sticking, denying the hard breach entirely. The MP7 is an elite SMG, so he can play aggressively around the wall and roam. Winning looks like the main breach wall never opening and the attack stalling for it.
**Flex note:** A pure-roam Bandit who batteries the wall and then hunts in deep is common and effective — the MP7 makes him a top fragger. Tricking the wall *and* roaming is the gold standard, but if a player wins rounds roaming aggressively, that's valid.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Anti-utility anchor **with** elite frag potential — **K/D undersells his job.** The real metric is breaches denied / Bandit-tricks landed, then frags as a bonus. Bad habit: batterying the wrong wall, or leaving the battery static so a Thatcher EMP kills it. Good habit: active tricking that defeats the breach, plus MP7 kills. *(Squad note: a Bandit who only frags and never tricks the wall is leaving his core value on the table.)*

### Castle
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** UMP45, M1014
**Secondary weapons:** 5.7 USG, Super Shorty
**Gadget:** Armor Panel — bulletproof barricades deployed on doors and windows, slowing attacker entry and channeling their approach.
**Recommended role:** Site flow control / anchor
**Recommended playstyle:** Panel the doors/windows that **aren't** your team's rotation or sightline, forcing attackers to spend time and utility breaking in elsewhere — funneling them into your team's crossfire. Never panel a hole your own team needs to shoot through or rotate to. The UMP45 holds angles fine. Winning looks like attackers burning time and tools to open panels while your team controls the channeled approach.
**Flex note:** Castle is genuinely flexible on which openings to seal map-to-map; there's no single correct setup. The mistake is sealing your *own* team's flow, not which doors he chooses.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Pure utility/setup — **K/D is a weak metric.** Judge time bought, attacker utility forced, and whether panels channeled the push into the team. Bad habit (the classic Castle flag): paneling a wall the team needs, or sealing a useless door that buys nothing — and worst of all, *over-playing Castle while a stronger anchor sits unused.* Good habit: panels that cost the attack real time/utility and shape the fight in the team's favor. *(Persistent coaching note: Castle being someone's most-played defender despite weak results is a setup-discipline problem, not an aim problem — fix the panel placement and the role priority first.)*

### Caveira

**Primary weapons:** M12, SPAS-15
**Secondary weapons:** Luison
**Gadget:** Silent Step + Interrogation — near-silent movement to flank undetected; downing an attacker lets her interrogate them, revealing all attacker positions to the team.
**Recommended role:** Roamer / assassin
**Recommended playstyle:** Move silently to flank isolated attackers, down one, and interrogate for a team-wide reveal that turns the round. The Luison's poison rounds make downs easy at close range. Patience is everything — wait for an attacker to isolate, don't sprint into a stack. Winning looks like a clean down-and-interrogate that exposes the whole attack.
**Flex note:** A more aggressive early-roam Caveira who pressures spawn/setup can work in the right hands, but it's high-variance — if the win rate holds, fine; if she's feeding, pull the timing back to patient flanks.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Roamer — K/D matters, but her signature value is the *interrogation reveal*, which often wins rounds with a single down. Bad habit: loud, impatient roams that trade her out early for nothing. Good habit: patient silent flanks, isolated downs, and interrogations at the moment they swing the round.

### Denari
**Side:** DEF
**Primary weapons:** Scorpion EVO 3 A1, FMG-9
**Secondary weapons:** Glaive-12 (slug shotgun), P226 MK 25
**Gadget:** T.R.I.P. Connector — seven throwable nodes that link via laser beams (up to ~20m apart with line of sight); attackers crossing a beam are slowed, take chip damage, and have crossing gadgets/drones destroyed. A blend of Aruni and Kapkan. He's a fast 3-speed operator.
**Recommended playstyle:** Spread connectors across roam-clear routes, staircases, and choke points to slow pushes, deny drone/gadget passage, and force attackers to waste utility clearing visible lasers. Use the Glaive-12 to open precise angles behind your laser walls and punish hesitating attackers. The Scorpion EVO frags hard. Winning looks like attackers stalled and chipped in laser-locked corridors while you trade from a prepared angle.
**Recommended role:** Trap / flex (anchor *or* roam)
**Flex note:** Denari genuinely supports both anchoring (connectors locking plant approaches) and roaming (connectors on roam routes, then hunt) thanks to his speed and aggressive guns. Either is valid — follow what the player's win rate rewards.
**VANTAGE coaching lens:** Trap/utility **with** strong frag upside — judge both. Round impact (routes denied, gadgets destroyed, time bought) is the core, but the Scorpion EVO means weak frags aren't fully excused. Bad habit: stacking connectors in one spot or placing them where attackers never go, then dying out of position. Good habit: a spread laser network that drains attacker time/utility, plus confident SMG trades.
